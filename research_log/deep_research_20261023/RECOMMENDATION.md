## 1. The concrete research question

**Which knapsack information should a quantum backtracking predicate evaluate coherently, given that stronger pruning can require a much more expensive circuit?**

For a fixed threshold, a stronger valid upper bound can make a smaller search tree. Quantum traversal, however, repeatedly evaluates predicates, reverses workspace and applies walk operations. A smaller tree alone is therefore insufficient to establish lower total resources. This principle is established in prior backtracking/resource literature; the proposed work must add knapsack-specific constructions, measured tree semantics, actual compiled circuits and a new reproducible finding.

Compare at least these predicates under the same exact variable order, integer data, threshold policy and capacity checks:

1. A simple remaining-profit bound.
2. One precompiled root-dual bound.
3. Exact residual fractional-knapsack LP pruning, implemented by integer cross products.
4. The preserved safely rounded fixed-point implementation as a reproducibility baseline.

The relevant baseline implementation is `simulation/oracle_h/node_bound_reversible.py`, not the restored paper's unestablished 96-qubit legacy claim. No superiority over the best published arithmetic circuit is currently established.

### Cheap bound: derivation and scope

For integer weights w_i>0, values v_i>=0, capacity W>=0 and a chosen nonnegative rational multiplier lambda=a/b, b>0, set r_i=b*v_i-a*w_i and A=a*W+sum_i max(r_i,0). Each prefix assignment incurs nonnegative loss D: assigning 0 where r_i>0 or 1 where r_i<0 incurs |r_i|. For any feasible completion, b*value <= A-D by weak duality. Therefore D>A-b*tau safely prunes at integer threshold tau. Equality must remain alive.

All coefficients are classically compiled constants; the coherent circuit adds prefix losses, checks capacity and compares integers. It needs no quantum division, approximate efficiency or floating-point order. This identity is standard Lagrangian/reduced-cost mathematics. The research question is whether its coherent implementation and larger tree yield useful total-resource regimes.

Keep explicit capacity rejection and actual leaf marking: a dual upper bound at a leaf is not itself the leaf's objective value. At tied densities the losses may vanish; objective pruning can then disappear. Nonoptimal nonnegative multipliers are safe but may be unhelpful. Negative multipliers are outside the argument.

### Exact LP alternative

At a feasible node, the exact residual fractional bound has form U=P+r*v_s/w_s. With w_s>0, U<tau iff P*w_s+r*v_s<tau*w_s. All-fit suffixes need a separate integer comparison, and overweight prefixes need explicit rejection. Correct ordering, sufficient register widths, coherent depth handling, strict comparison and cleanup are required.

The arithmetic identity is elementary. It removes a precision complication but is not itself a new theorem or a guarantee of a smaller circuit. The probes already falsify the stronger claim that precision removal always lowers resource usage.

### Small dual bank and static certificates

The saved tree-only bank pilot uses the root multiplier, optionally 0, and optionally extreme densities, with duplicates removed. The nominal four-entry bank has either two or four distinct multipliers in this pilot. Caps fall from 11 to 9 to 8 across nominal bank sizes 1,2,4. On the 79 conditions where the one-bound baseline completes, median node reductions are about 1.32 and 1.66 for nominal two- and four-bound banks (DERIVED). Circuit costs have not been measured or compiled for these banks. More bounds are not automatically a win: the saved tree reductions may be too small to repay extra arithmetic.

The direct static-prefix certificate counts low-penalty prefixes; it must include generated rejected children if the tree representation retains them. A conservative count is 1+2*sum_{d=0}^{n-1}N_d(H), with H=A-b*tau, capped by 2^(n+1)-1. H<0 is a rejected root and still counts one vertex. A cutoff/binomial envelope is cheap to compute, but may be loose. Exact subset-count DP is pseudopolynomial in its integer budget and must not be hidden as free preprocessing.

The cheap envelope was smaller than the full binary-tree bound in 75/90 pilot conditions, including 11 root-rejected cases; this is not by itself evidence of an important asymptotic improvement. Certificate costs and tightness must be compared. Using a feasible incumbent L, a certificate at L+1 can cover value-search calls at tau>=L+1 and compatible witness subtrees only if original variable order and cumulative prefix penalties are preserved. Save the incumbent witness for optimum=L. Do not import best-bound-first optimization-tree theorems unchanged into these static trees.

## 7. Directions not selected for the critical path

- **Quantum game theory graft:** changes a single optimizer into a strategic multi-agent problem and needs new equilibrium/information assumptions and fair correlated-strategy baselines. Novelty, coherence and implementation risks are substantially higher. Retain as a separate topic, not a novelty label.
- **Quantum Zeno, thermodynamic or sensing decoration:** no source-backed low-risk connection to the present technical question was established. Do not add a topic merely because the journal covers it.
- **A new asymptotic advantage theorem:** can be valuable but cannot be promised feasible. Use existing theorems correctly; keep the new small certificate an optional transparent specialization, not a guaranteed breakthrough.
- **A full large LP-bounded walk on free IBM hardware:** current candidate workspace/depth gives no basis to promise this. Compile and validate small components first.
- **Noise mitigation alone or another tiny Grover run:** established ideas; need a distinct controlled finding and enough evidence. Sampling a known marked state is not an optimization algorithm.
- **Training a predictor of query budgets built from tree size:** circular validation. Learn no physical scaling claim from a construction policy.
