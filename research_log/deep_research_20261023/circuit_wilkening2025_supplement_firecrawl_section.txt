## F. Benchmarking of competitive quantum methods

This section contains a brief overview and runtime estimations of the nested quantum search [11] and quantum
branch-and-bound [12, 13] algorithms, together with our
benchmarking reasoning.

## 1. Nested Quantum Search

Nested quantum search (NQS) is a nested version of
Grover’s algorithm specifically tailored to constraint satisfaction problems, where the search Hilbert space is decomposed into two factors H<sub>S</sub> = H<sub>A</sub> ⊗H<sub>B</sub>, corresponding to a decomposition S = A *∪* B of the set of classical
variables. The algorithm itself is a variant of QSearch,
where the superposition-creating unitary *A* consists of
the following two steps:

$$
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{S}}=\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{A}}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{B}}
$$

$$

(nearly) obtain a uniform superposition of *could-be*
solutions, i.e. partial assignments (only variables
in A are assigned) that fulfill all constraints that
are testable on these variables. Accordingly, these
are precisely the partial assignments that *could be*
extended to a full solution of the problem. The employed oracle checks whether a partial assignment
is a could-be solution and is assumed to only act
on H<sub>A</sub>.

## 2. Quantum branch-and-bound

Quantum branch-and-bound (QBnB) [12, 13] is one of
the most promising candidates for solving ILPs, having a
provable worst-case asymptotic near-quadratic speedup
over its classical counterpart. The near-quadratic speedup is achieved by using quantum walks [15] on the tree
explored by the classical algorithm. The method has two
main ingredients:

1. Quantum tree size estimation [16] to get an upper
bound on the size of the tree. This implementation
shows how to get a quadratic improvement over a
classical backtracking algorithm which evaluates *T*
nodes of a search tree.

$$
T
$$

2. Quantum tree search [15] to return the label of
the marked node if the tree contains at least one
marked node, or “not found” if there is no marked
node.

In general, the QBnB routine requires multiple applications of quantum tree size estimation and quantum tree
search. At the core of these subroutines lies quantum
backtracking [16–18]. In the following, we give an optimistic runtime estimation of the QBnB method. We
benchmark the QBnB for the 0-1-KP under the following assumptions:

1. The optimal solution and the respective tree size *T*
are known, hence quantum tree size estimation has
to be applied only once.

2. The success probability of the quantum subroutines
is 1.

3. The most expensive part of the algorithm is the
backtracking, hence we consider all the other quantum subroutines to be queried once.

In general, in order to find the optimal solution, the
*√*
expected number of oracles calls is at least *Tn,* where
*T* represents the number of nodes that are actually explored by the corresponding classical BnB algorithm, and
*n* is the number of items. To justify this value, we give
a summary of the backtracking algorithm together with
our reasoning. Consider a rooted tree with *T* vertices
*r,1,...,T −* 1, where *r* is the root. *A* denotes the set
of vertices at an even distance from *r,* and *B* the set of
vertices at an odd distance from *r. x → y* signifies that
*y* is a child of *x* in the tree. The quantum walk operates
in the space spanned by *{|x⟩* : *x ∈ {r,1,...,T − 1}}*
and starts in the state *|r⟩.* This quantum walk utilises
a set of diffusion operators D<sub>x</sub>, where D<sub>x</sub> is the identity
operator if *x* is a solution; otherwise, it diffuses on the
subspace spanned by *{|x⟩}∪{|y⟩* : *x → y}.* A step of
the quantum walk involves applying the operator R<sub>B</sub>R<sub>A</sub>,

Within the BnB routine, we use the commonly used
upper bound heuristic *Fractional Greedy* [19] which, similar to (Integer) Greedy, sorts all items by decreasing density and then greedily packs items into the knapsack until
an item does not fit into the knapsack (by definition the
successor of the break item h). It then returns the sum
of all the profits of contained items plus the profit of the
fraction of the item that could be included additionally,
i.e.

$$
\sum_{m=1}^{h}p_{m}+p_{h+1}\frac{c-\sum_{m=1}^{h}w_{m}}{w_{h+1}}.
$$
