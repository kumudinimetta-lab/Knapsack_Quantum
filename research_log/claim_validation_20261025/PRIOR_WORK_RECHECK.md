**Bożejko, Burduk, Pempera, Uchroński and Wodecki (2024), _Optimal solving of a binary knapsack problem on a D-Wave quantum machine and its implementation in production systems_, Annals of Operations Research, DOI [10.1007/s10479-024-06025-1](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10479-024-06025-1).** Primary [PDF](https://d-nb.info/1336507756/34), Section 3.1, equations (16)–(20), and algorithm in Section 4. With their multiplier u=-lambda, equation (17)'s maximization over binary items gives the same affine Lagrangian upper bound. Their branch-and-bound algorithm also floors upper bounds before incumbent pruning. Therefore neither the bound formula, a minimum over multipliers, nor integer-floor pruning is new here. Their node-level annealing formulation does not itself establish the proposed static all-prefix coverage compiler, cardinality certificate or clean reversible implementation. No performance comparison to their device experiment was performed. Do not import their loosely stated Lemma 1 without its maximization context; equation (17) is the unambiguous object compared. Primary text is cached at `.firecrawl/claim_validation_dwave_knapsack2024.md`.

**Wilkening et al. (2025), _A quantum algorithm for solving 0-1 Knapsack problems_, npj Quantum Information 11, 146, DOI [10.1038/s41534-025-01097-8](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41534-025-01097-8).** Primary [arXiv version](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.06623v2), Appendix A.6.2 (journal Supplement F.2): fractional greedy pruning in quantum branch-and-bound is already present. Appendix arithmetic/scheduling uses QFT components and parallelization choices. Our reconstructed LP and bank netlists compare our own matched implementations; they do not establish superiority to this released algorithm or its optimized scheduling. The R1 call-path audit remains separately preserved.

**Hamitouche, Hnaien and Menacer (2025), _Towards a Quantum Resolution of the Knapsack Problem_, PRICAI workshop proceedings, CDMTCS-586, pp. 18 onward of the [primary proceedings PDF](https://www.cs.auckland.ac.nz/research/groups/CDMTCS/researchreports/download.php?selected-id=904).** Sections 4–5 construct a reversible capacity/value feasibility oracle using QFT arithmetic and apply a separate classical fractional bound at a branch-and-bound node. This is prior evidence against claiming generic reversible knapsack oracles, QFT ancilla reuse, or a hybrid quantum/classical bound scheme as new. The described classical bound and complete-assignment oracle are different objects from compiling a coherent partial-prefix LP-pruning predicate. No theorem or performance claim from this workshop paper is relied on here.

LP duality and greedy interval stabbing also remain standard ingredients, already attributed in the derivation. A general ability to reversibly compute a classical predicate is not, by itself, a convincing novelty claim. The potentially distinct package is: all-prefix/all-integer-threshold coverage compilation under a fixed density order; sufficient DP-free and certified minimum-cardinality variants; a worst-case cardinality limitation; and explicit clean netlists with resource tradeoffs. Historical priority and journal-level significance of that package remain **UNRESOLVED**.
