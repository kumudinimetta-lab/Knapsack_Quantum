# Scope, rejected statements, and remaining work

23 October 2026. This is the research phase. The restored manuscript and bibliography remain unchanged. Their previously logged scientific/citation issues are still pending the subsequent manuscript-editing phase.

## What is implemented

Explicit X/CX/CCX compute-copy-uncompute pruning circuits for a simple profit bound, a root-dual loss bound, small nested multiplier banks, an exact residual fractional-LP comparison, and safe fixed-point controls. Coherent depth and threshold are inputs in the broad production comparison. A separate fixed-depth compact ripple-arithmetic pilot controls compiler sensitivity on matching interfaces. Classical threshold-tree enumeration and value/witness reconstruction are structural accounting; a gate-level full walk, reflection, detection, or quantum optimizer has not been implemented here.

The evidence supports constructions and scoped resource/structural observations. It does not establish measured quantum runtime, asymptotic algorithmic improvement, practical quantum advantage, best possible circuit costs, hardware readiness, or a journal acceptance probability. No IBM job was submitted.

## Statements rejected before paper drafting

1. **First LP-pruned quantum branch-and-bound for knapsack.** Rejected: Wilkening et al. (2025), Supplement F.2 explicitly includes fractional-greedy LP pruning and quantum BnB resource estimates. Its assumptions and the narrower possible construction-level difference are recorded in `WILKENING_SUPPLEMENT_OVERLAP.md`. Generic heuristic-cost/pruning tradeoffs also precede this study in Martiel and Remaud.
2. **A sufficient precision inequality is an instancewise iff condition.** Rejected: a conservative error envelope can exceed a rational-gap floor while an individual instance has zero truncation error. For example, weights [2,2], profits [2,2], capacity 1 and k=0 have integral efficiencies, hence no efficiency truncation error, although the uniform envelope wmax/2^k=2 exceeds 1/wmax=1/2. This disproves necessity of that envelope condition on individual instances. The existing safe fixed-point construction uses a proved sufficient condition, exact order, a margin-aware comparator and nonoverflowing widths. Exact cross products are an alternative with no truncation. Neither arithmetic identity is claimed new.
3. **Exhaustive dense statevector simulation of all 700 large circuits.** Not performed and not claimed. Hundreds or thousands of workspace qubits prevent dense allocation. Tiny cases exhaust selected clean-input domains using exact permutation-column propagation; the 700-input audit tests recorded prefixes/thresholds at every encoded depth and both target bits. Column simulation executes every emitted gate; finite samples are not exhaustive verification of every large input state. Dense Qiskit checks are separately scoped and capped. Derivation and adversarial tests support general reasoning, but do not turn sampled large-domain checks into exhaustive tests.
4. **Every safe dual predicate should equal exact LP pruning.** Rejected: weaker bounds can safely retain extra nodes. Own-bound predicate agreement and independent no-unsafe-prune checks are different metrics. Exact and safe fixed-point predicates should agree on integer threshold rejection under their stated conditions.
5. **Circuit cost times sqrt(T) is an experimental quantum speedup.** Rejected: it is a derived conditional proxy using a classically enumerated tree. The full walk, leaf marking, branching, reflections and success/error amplification are not included in predicate gate lists. Common overhead sensitivity is an explicit model, not a measured completion of those circuits. Scheduled certificate-based detection scales are not measured queries and are not independently validated by regression on T.
6. **Uniformly scaled inputs prove robustness on intrinsically difficult high-bit instances.** Rejected: gcd normalization removes the global scale. Thresholds must also respect the objective lattice; capacity normalizes by floor division and threshold by ceil division. Independent R=1,000,000 data are a separate panel with an independently recomputed exact MitM reference.
7. **A common root certificate is the tight bank-specific tree size.** Rejected: the certificate is an upper bound. Taking the minimum of valid member certificates is another valid bound, not exact counting of their intersection. Certification time and looseness are retained. Actual-T comparisons and certificate-based schedules remain separate.
8. **Our leaf-only fixed-order tree is optimized classical BnB.** Rejected: a strong classical solver can return integral-LP completions or use other early-success rules, node ordering and incumbent updates. These experimental trees implement a chosen static family with matching acceptance semantics. Completion rates under diagnostic caps are not comparative quantum/classical runtime results.
9. **Compact arithmetic improves every resource.** Rejected by the pilot: reusable registers reduce qubits while increasing gates and serial depth. Both effects remain in the report. Neither compiler is asserted optimal.
